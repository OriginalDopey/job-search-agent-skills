# Career Coaching Frameworks — Reference

> Four structured frameworks for anchoring career direction, weekly reflection, and networking. These are standard career-coaching techniques adapted for an AI-assisted job search workflow.

---

## 1. Career Motivators (14 Motivators / 25 Points / Top 3)

**Method:** Read all 14 motivator descriptions. Allocate a total of **25 points** across all of them based on their relative importance *to you right now*. Then pick your **Top 3** and capture (a) what each means in your own words and (b) what you could do to satisfy it more.

### The 14 Motivators

| # | Motivator | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Work Intensity** | Long hours, fast pace, juggling multiple projects, tight deadlines energize you. |
| 2 | **Continuity** | Stable environment, security, solid-reputation organization, consistent workload. |
| 3 | **Expertise** | Specialized work aligned with your interests; refining skills/knowledge; high-specialist roles. |
| 4 | **Reward & Recognition** | Acknowledgment drives you; demotivated when these are lacking. |
| 5 | **Enterprise & Risk** | Calculated risks, entrepreneurial environments, freedom to experiment, comfort with uncertainty. |
| 6 | **Variety** | Diverse tasks, work that doesn't repeat, varied experiences. |
| 7 | **Meaning & Purpose** | Positive impact, values-aligned work, willing to accept lower pay for principle alignment. |
| 8 | **Autonomy** | Control over your work and decisions, flexible/self-directed setting. |
| 9 | **Power & Influence** | Decisions with impact, leadership/authority, comfortable with unpopular-but-necessary calls. |
| 10 | **Relationships** | Close working relationships, social aspect, team-oriented environments. |
| 11 | **Work-Life Blend** | Healthy balance, flexibility, personal life takes precedence. |
| 12 | **Creativity** | Innovative solutions, freedom to experiment, expression in work. |
| 13 | **Learning & Stretch** | New challenges, continuous learning, skill growth, career development. |
| 14 | **Work Environment** | Positive culture, well-being, leaders with shared values, productivity-supportive systems. |

### Career Crafting (3 Modes)

Once you know your top 3, "career crafting" lets you reshape your current role or next role to better satisfy them. Three modes:

| Mode | What it Changes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Task crafting** | The type or amount of work you do | Volunteer for projects, delegate things you don't enjoy, add new tasks, become more efficient |
| **Cognitive crafting** | How you see your work / connection to values | Reframe how your work makes a difference, surface under-used talents, set boundaries, review where your influence matters |
| **Relational crafting** | Kinds of interactions with others | Active listening, seek feedback, share knowledge / mentor, develop visibility |

### How This Anchors Job Search

When evaluating a role, run it past your Top 3 motivators:
- Does the JD/comp/team description support each of the 3?
- If a role is a great-fit on paper but doesn't align with motivators, expect dissatisfaction within 6 months.
- If a role partially aligns, identify which motivator is the gap and decide if it's negotiable (Task / Cognitive / Relational crafting from day one).

## 2. Career Story Timeline

**Method:** Take 30 minutes to plot the highlights and low points of your career on a +4 to -4 scale, chronologically. Then reflect on patterns and plan forward.

### The Process

1. **Sketch the timeline.** Plot key moments, projects, learning situations, people you worked with, non-work experiences, career breaks.
2. **Reflect on highlights:** "During that career highlight, I enjoyed the sense that I... <one or two words>." (e.g., "had variety in my day-to-day work" or "was making a difference to the profitability of the business" or "had autonomy")
3. **Reflect on low points:** "During that low point/challenge, I felt dissatisfied because... and I learned... <one or two words>." (e.g., "the situation felt really unfair... to focus on the things I can change or influence rather than dwell on things outside my control")
4. **Analyze themes and patterns:**
   - What themes do you notice about your career? How has it evolved?
   - When and where have you been most satisfied/dissatisfied? What influenced this? Which projects stand out?
   - How much control have you had over your career? What or who else has influenced it?
5. **Plan your future career story:**
   - What factors from your past will influence your next decisions?
   - What does your story so far tell you about what's important to you now?
   - What would you like to have more of?
   - What do you want to do less of / leave behind?

### How This Anchors Job Search

Use the Career Story Timeline to:
- Identify your repeatable "satisfaction conditions" (what made past highlights satisfying)
- Filter target roles by whether they reproduce those conditions
- Spot patterns that no longer serve you (e.g., "every time I took a role for the comp jump without the autonomy, I burned out")
- Choose direction over chasing isolated opportunities

## 3. Map Your Network

**Method:** Visual record of people who matter to your goal. Each contact gets classified across **Strength × Importance × Type** with a Next Step.

### The Classifications

**Strength** (your relationship to the person):
- **Strong** — regular contact, know each other well
- **Medium** — occasional contact, don't know each other well
- **Weak** — met once or twice, barely know each other

**Importance** (to your current networking objective):
- **Very** — high relevance to the goal
- **Reasonably** — adjacent help possible
- **Not very** — limited connection to goal

**Type of support** (can be multiple):
- **Informational** — understands how things work, what's expected, skills needed for the future
- **Structural** — can help you become visible / known to key stakeholders, well-positioned for new opportunities
- **Emotional** — invested in your happiness and success, encouragement, helping overcome obstacles

### The 8-Step Process

1. Define your networking objective (single sentence).
2. List your connections (friends, family, acquaintances, past/present colleagues).
3. Describe what each relationship offers (ideas, advice, information, intros).
4. Assess relationship Strength.
5. Prioritize by Importance to your objective.
6. Categorize Type of support.
7. Make notes on the Next Step for each contact.
8. Reflect on balance — across all 3 Types, are any underrepresented?

### Reciprocity Principle

Networking is most effective and satisfying when reciprocal. For every ask, surface a return-favor or contribution.

## 4. Learning Journal — Weekly Check-In Pattern

A six-section weekly reflection structure for career transition:

| Section | Reflection Questions |
|---|---|
| **Focus** | Is there something missing from your career right now? What is your top priority? |
| **Goals** | What do you need to do now to identify and create a fulfilling career story? What will help you achieve your goals? |
| **Strengths** | How clear are you about your strengths, personal skills and experiences? How can you ensure that your future includes more of the things that you enjoy and do well? |
| **Exploring Possibilities** | What do you hope to share or learn (via discussion / forum / mentor)? How can you tap into your network to reflect ideas and gain support? |
| **Removing Barriers** | How can you make sure you set aside enough time? What are the possible assumptions or self-imposed limitations that may hold you back? |
| **Personal Notes** | How are you feeling about completing this experience? What is emerging as a theme for your career? |

**How to use:** Run this every Friday as a weekly review. Pair with the Job Tracker pipeline check.

**Where to capture:** `../tracker/Weekly-Review.md` (template for each weekly entry)

## 5. Informational Interviewing

The single most effective networking technique for job searching. Different from a job interview — no one is evaluating you for a role.

**What it is:** A 20–30 minute structured conversation with someone who works at a target company or in a target function. You ask questions about their work, career path, and company. They share. You build a relationship and surface opportunities you'd never find on a job board.

**Why it works:** 70–80% of jobs are filled through networking, not job boards. Informational interviews surface those roles before they're posted.

**The 5-step process:**

1. **Identify the target.** Someone at a company on your target list, a former colleague at a target company, or an alumni connection.

2. **Send a specific, short outreach** (use `../skills/network-mapper.md` template for the wording):
   > "Hi [Name] — I'm a [your title/specialty] transitioning to my next chapter. I see you're at [Company], which I'm researching. I'd love 20 minutes of your perspective on how [Company] approaches [your domain]. Not asking for a job — just value your viewpoint. Worth connecting?"

3. **Structure the conversation (20–30 min):**
   - Opening (2 min): "Tell me how you got to your current role" — gets them talking
   - Their work (5 min): "What's the biggest challenge on your plate right now?"
   - Your pitch (5 min): Brief career summary, adapted to their context
   - Close (5 min): "Who else would you suggest I talk to?" / "Would you be open to staying in touch?"

4. **Follow up within 24 hours.** Brief thank-you note referencing one specific insight from the conversation. Connect on LinkedIn.

5. **Target: 2 informational conversations per week** during active job search. This is the Weekly Review Network Touches metric.

**Standard questions to cycle through:**
- "How did you end up at [Company]? What drew you here?"
- "What does a successful [role title] look like at your company?"
- "What's the biggest shift you've seen in [your field] in the last 2 years?"
- "If you were transitioning into your function from the outside, what would you wish you knew?"
- "Who else at [Company] or in [field] do you think I should connect with?"

## 6. Putting it All Together — The Operating Cadence

Combine the four frameworks into one operating rhythm:

```
Once  → Career Story Timeline (one-time deep reflection)
Once  → Career Motivators (re-run every 6 months)
Once  → Map Your Network (initial map)
Weekly → Learning Journal entry (Friday)
Weekly → Pipeline check via job-tracker skill
Weekly → Network review — anyone stale or missing a Type?
Per-role → role-fit-rater + tailored-resume-builder + tracker update
```

The skills in `../skills/` automate the per-role and weekly steps. The tracker files in `../tracker/` hold the data. This file is the methodology reference.
