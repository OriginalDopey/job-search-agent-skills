# LinkedIn Update Playbook

> Step-by-step guide for refreshing your LinkedIn profile during a job transition. Do these in order. Write your own copy for each step — the structure tells you what to include.

---

## Step 1: Update Headline

Your headline is the first thing recruiters see in search results. Format:

```
[Current/Target Title] | [2-3 Keyword Themes] | [Notable Platform/Cert] | Ex-[Company]
```

**Tips:**
- Hit the keywords recruiters search for in your domain
- "Ex-[Company]" signals availability without saying "laid off"
- Cover both your primary and secondary audiences (e.g., both "Engineering Manager" and "Platform Architecture")

---

## Step 2: Update Current/Most Recent Experience Section

**Fields:**
- Title: [Your actual title]
- Company: [Company name]
- Location: [City, State (Remote if applicable)]
- Dates: [Start] – [End or Present]

**Description:** Write a 6-bullet executive version. LinkedIn's experience section works best at this density — detailed enough to prove the work, scannable enough that someone reads it in 30 seconds.

Structure:
1. **Opening paragraph (2-3 sentences):** Your scope — team size, org size, what you owned.
2. **Bullets (4-6):** One major accomplishment each. Lead with outcome and metric. Same rules as resume bullets (outcomes over stack, varied lead verbs).

---

## Step 3: Update About Section

LinkedIn's About section allows ~2,600 characters. Structure:

1. **Opening identity line** (who you are, in what context)
2. **2-3 paragraphs** covering your top achievements with metrics
3. **Closing line** signaling openness ("exploring my next chapter" / "happy to connect")

**Voice:** First-person, conversational but professional. No "passionate about" or "leveraging." Write like you'd talk to a peer at a conference.

---

## Step 4: Update Pinned Skills (top 5)

Reorder your skills to put these at the top:
1. [Your primary domain skill]
2. [Your secondary domain skill]
3. [Your key platform/tool]
4. [A differentiator skill]
5. [A technical skill]

Add any that are missing. These affect search ranking.

---

## Step 5: Turn On "Open to Work"

**Settings > Privacy > Job seeking preferences > Let recruiters know you're open**

Set:
- **Job titles:** [List 4-6 titles you'd accept]
- **Location:** [Your metro area + Remote]
- **Start date:** Immediately (or your actual availability)
- **Job types:** Full-time, Contract (whatever applies)
- **Visibility:** "Recruiters only" — keep the green banner off your photo. Let a thought-leadership post do the public signaling instead.

---

## Step 6: Consider LinkedIn Premium

During an active job search, Premium Career gives you:
- InMail credits (reach recruiters directly)
- Who viewed your profile (follow up with them)
- Salary insights on job postings
- "Featured Applicant" badge on applications

Worth it during active search. Cancel after you land. Usually has a 1-month free trial.

---

## Step 7: Post a Thought-Leadership Signal

Post something that demonstrates your expertise AND signals availability. Best timing: Tuesday–Thursday 8–10 AM in your timezone.

**Effective formats:**
- A checklist of "what I actually do" (shows breadth)
- A short case study from your recent work (shows depth)
- A contrarian take on your field (shows thinking)
- A "looking for my next chapter" post with specific keywords

**Close with:** a clear call-to-action. "DMs open" or "Happy to connect" or "Looking for [specific thing]."

**Include hashtags:** #OpenToWork + 2-3 domain-specific ones.

---

## Step 8: Add Certifications

List all current certifications in the Licenses & Certifications section. These are searchable by recruiters.

---

## Step 9: Add Awards/Honors

Under "Honors & Awards" — include anything from your career that shows external recognition.

---

## Timing

- Steps 1-5: Do today (takes ~45 minutes)
- Step 6: Optional, do if budget allows
- Step 7: Schedule for Tuesday-Thursday morning
- Steps 8-9: Do while you're already in the settings

---

*The goal is to make your profile searchable, scannable, and signal-rich within one sitting. Write your own copy — don't paste AI-generated text into LinkedIn without editing it to sound like you.*
